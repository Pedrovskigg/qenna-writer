**Qenna Writer**
**HUGE Update: 0.18.0 is here!**

Surprise, fellas!

When you least expect it, here it comes, sneaking in, quiet as can be...
A NEW **UPDATE** FOR QENNA WRITER!
The one and only! The incredible, wonderful app I built for writing, which somehow became my favorite way to procrastinate on **not** writing!

And this update goes back to the basics, bringing new tools and a bunch of new options, customizations and fixes.
The UI and the overall feel of the app went through a wild evolution. Loads of new tools, options, features and customization!
I think this is the biggest patch note I've ever written in this app's history. And it's not because I ramble, you know? It's because there's genuinely a ton of stuff.

Come take a closer look:

## What's new

We've got some pretty cool new toys in the sandbox, check it out:

**• Outline Mode**
A new, dockable panel.
Through it, read, drag and organize your manuscript's scenes and chapters as interactive cards.
Track where characters show up, reorganize scenes and the order of the story, freely.

**• Timeline rework**
Third time's the charm, and probably the final one.
The Timeline had one problem, and I admit it was a bit selfish of me:
*I was the only one who liked its design... yeah. I know.*
Visually, it was ugly, confusing, weird to use. And that *pushed people away* from the tool, making it look more complex than it really is.
So, that's it.
The Timeline went through a **COMPLETE** rework.
It's now visually beautiful, simple to understand, interactive as always, customizable and, of course, still fully automated and functional like it always was.
Trust me, you're going to love it.
But for the nostalgic ones out there... don't worry. The legacy Timeline is still available in the panel.

However, this Timeline feature opened a gap I wasn't expecting. It got so pretty that... it made the rest of the app look kind of dated.
And that bugged me enough for everything below to happen.
It's a new era of customization for Qenna, and trust me, it's pretty great. Check it out:

**• New color picker tool**
Any function in Qenna that needed to change a color — whether a simple marker or creating a theme — used to open a HUGE, HIDEOUS window that looked like a Windows XP Paint color palette. It was ugly, disconnected from the app's UI, and just bad.
Now, Qenna has a new color tool. It's called up by any function in the app that needs a palette. Intuitive, easy to use, and it doesn't eat up 40% of your screen.

**• Doc Header - Drawers**
In the last version, we implemented the new Doc Header (the strip at the top of the page showing the name of the document you're editing).
Now, it goes even deeper in drawers. It also shows the character's role and photo, for example.
And you can also double-click the photo in the Doc Header to change it quickly. No photo yet? Hover over it and a spot to add the first one shows up.
Note: I used characters as the example, but this works for any document with a narrative element (settings, objects).

**• Image cropping**
Inserting an image into the text now has the option to crop it first. Cropped it wrong? Undo the crop and start over, always from the original image.

**• Documents bar**
The documents bar (left bar for close friends) got a really nice visual update. It now has animations, both during interaction and when opening drawers. Drawers slide out from behind the bar, and the content cascades in.
It also got names now: hover the mouse over the bar and it shows the name of each button and how many items are in each drawer. No more guessing which icon is which drawer. Some people prefer the bar clean, I know. You can turn it off in the settings.

**• Project drawer**
The Info button (the "i" at the top of the bar) is no longer that little form window. Now it opens a project drawer: cover, name, author, genres, synopsis, the project's numbers (books, words, characters), and the books in the saga.
Everything editable right there. Clicked the name, type away. Clicked the cover, change it. And it saves on its own, no OK button.

**• Selection menu**
The selection menu also got the same kind of tune-up as the left bar. It now has animations too, and went through a visual rework, organizing the tools more clearly by category.

**• Writing like in Word**
This one's my favorite of the whole update, seriously.
You know when you write in Word and the letters don't just pop onto the screen, they seem to *glide*? I never knew how to explain why. Until now: it's the cursor. It doesn't jump from one letter to the next, it glides. And the new letter fades in quickly, instead of just appearing.
Well, Qenna does that now. And the difference in how writing feels is wild.
And since everyone has their own taste: in the settings, you choose the speed of the cursor glide and the letter fade, and there's even a line right there to test it before going back to your text. Not your thing? Turn it off and the old cursor comes back.

**• Animations, animations everywhere**
Qenna used to be a dry app. You'd click a drawer and... blink. It would just appear.
Not anymore. Pensarium, Stats, the Reference Menu, Mira, Immersive Sound and Reminders now enter and exit with animation, each one coming from wherever makes sense. And every window that opens when you use something (creating a memory, a marker, an event, settings...) does too.
For those who prefer everything still: there's a toggle to turn off animations in the settings. No judgment. Okay, maybe a little.

**• New main menu**
We reworked the UI for pretty much everything... except the most important part: the first screen you see after opening the app. Yeah. Until now, whoever opened Qenna for the first time was met with an empty screen with two gray lines. And whoever already had projects saw giant generic covers with zero information. Not when you last touched it, not where you left off, nothing.
Not anymore. The Library now has new views:
**Continue** (the default): your last project front and center, with the chapter and last sentence of where you stopped, today's goal and your streak. The Continue button opens the project right at the last sentence you wrote. It's open the app, go back to writing.
**Showcase**: your covers in a grid, with genre, word count and when you last touched them. With search and sorting.
**Cinema**: the project's cover blown up big, blurred behind itself. Like a streaming service's home screen.
**Your day**: your projects on one side, and on the other, today's goal, your streak, the week and today's reminders.
**Shelf**: real shelves, with the current book pulled forward on the desk.
And the **Stack** is still there, for sentimental value. The old Shelf view and the List view have retired. "Open automatically" moved from under the covers to the right-click menu, along with the rest of the project's options.
First time on Qenna? Now there's a real welcome screen, with clear paths to get started, language, and a few quick themes to pick right there.

**• Customization: New Counters**
The Word Counter and Daily Goal got a really nice update: now it's customizable.
You can change its size and style. There are almost 20 different ones to choose from. From mini options (way smaller than the standard one) to others with more detail about your progress and goals hit. Like Week, which shows the days you wrote most that week right on the counter.
It's pretty fun, great for mixing with themes and making Qenna even more the way you want it.

**• Customization: Reference Menu**
Following the same idea the new counters brought, the Reference Menu let loose too. Now there are several different layouts for it. Gallery, Binder, Rail, Dock, Open Book. Pick whichever works best for you! Rail became the default, but you can switch anytime.
On top of that: docs can now be opened in tabs, so you can have several different ones open at the same time. You can also open more than one document side by side now.
And there's the Usage Map: a table with characters and places on one side and chapters on the other, showing who shows up where. Click it, it opens the chapter or the sheet. Great for finding that character who vanished from the book in chapter 4 and nobody noticed.

**• Customization: Manuscript Drawer**
In the spirit of the Reference Menu. The manuscript drawer now also has different swappable layouts. All kinds. With more or less information, illustrated, plain, indexes. Everything.
A special highlight is the **Illustrated Contents**, which generates a small, random, swappable illustration. This illustration **evolves** as the chapter progresses, becoming deeper and more detailed. It's beautiful.
Every chapter has its own, and no two are alike. Don't like the drawing? Right-click and swap it: branches, flames, city, constellation, mountains... There are 13 types, for a single chapter or for the whole book. And if you want, you can put your own image in there instead.
Other highlights: **Seasons** mode, which treats your book like a streaming series (each book is a season, each chapter an episode, with the same thumbnail art as Illustrated Contents), plus the Rail and Showcase modes. But there's a lot more: Title Page, Store Page, Saga Box Set, Reader... Pick yours.

**• Also in Manuscript: new tools**
Pace charts: analysis of dialogue vs. narration in the text and chapter length, giving a sense of your book's pacing swings.
Production status: mark chapters as draft, revised or final, and track how much of the book is done.
Where I left off: the drawer remembers the last place you wrote, with the last sentence. One click and you're back.
Parts: group chapters into parts or acts, each with a name and color.
POV: mark each chapter's narrator and see each one's line across the book.
Story order: switch between the order the book is read in and the order things actually happen. For those who love a flashback.
Revision: in Reader mode, the drawer tracks how far you've gotten through each chapter. Started revising the book? Reset and track the revision from start to finish.

**• Customization: Drawers**
Drawers now also have several different display styles. Image-forward, content-forward, information-forward. There are several designs to pick from, all functional and good-looking.
Now, character drawers also have tools for character presence analysis.
And more: hover over an item and see the essentials of the sheet without opening it. Compare two sheets side by side (right-click, Compare with). And to create a bond between two characters, just drag one's face onto the other's.

**• Customization: Pensarium**
Following the same lead as the previous ones, Pensarium also got this visual update. With new swappable display/UI models.

**• World Builder: now it's an Encyclopedia**
I loved making this one too.
The World Builder (Territories and the World Builder's Systems) used to live squeezed into a 280px rail next to the editor. List, spectrum, Favors/Demands and the rule tree, all crammed in there. It worked, but it hurt your eyes.
Now it's a real window. A toggle at the top switches between Places and Systems, and each territory or system becomes a continuous entry: title, summary and each rule or document as its own section (rules numbered like Art. 1, 1.1, just like actual law). Click any passage and write right there, no more opening one node at a time.
On top of that, every territory got a sheet on the side: its neighbors (with the bond between them), who was born or lives there (pulled straight from character sheets), which systems apply to that place, and what's already happened there on the Timeline.
If you use the World Builder for an epic fantasy series, this is going to make your life a lot easier.

**• Task creation**
When creating comments, you can now mark them as tasks, and check them from Pensarium.

Page glow: instead of casting a shadow, the page can spread light around itself. Light Table and Arc Light use this, go check it out.

**• Themes with English names**
48 themes still had Portuguese names. For anyone using the app in another language, it was impossible to find these themes through search. Now they have English names. (And yes, Pátina, Borra and Musgo that you knew are now Patina, Grounds and Moss. Same themes, new names.)
If you were using any of them, relax: the theme and your favorites are still there.

## Fixes

Repairs to keep everything running.

**• Ghost manuscript**
This could happen. The app would lose the manuscript's id. So chapters became orphaned and disappeared from the app. They weren't actually lost, they still existed in the project folder, but the app couldn't find them.
Now, the app checks and matches chapter ids against manuscript ids. If something's off, it fixes it.

**• Persistent panels**
Fixed a bug where minimizing the app with certain panels open could leave the panel on screen, even with the app minimized.

**• Update installer**
You know that "The installer was unable to automatically close all applications" message, with an "Ignore" button next to it, that would sometimes show up when updating? That was Qenna still finishing closing while the installer was already trying to work.
Now the installer waits for Qenna to actually close before it starts. No message, no leftover file. Fixed.

**• Data that would disappear**
Two situations where Qenna would forget things you had filled in:
A chapter with just one scene would lose the scene's title, time marker and summary.
And changes made only to a chapter or manuscript's data (without touching the text) might not save when closing the app.
Both fixed.

**• Timeline: branch name**
The Timeline's branching warning showed a strange code instead of the branch's name. Fixed.

**• Drawer icons in the Reference Menu**
In some colorful themes, the drawer icon would simply disappear in the Reference Menu. Now every drawer shows its icon in its own color.

**• Colorless New Project button**
The New Project button in the main menu was always supposed to have the theme's accent color. A bug left it gray (and took the accent color from other parts of the menu with it). Fixed. It now has your theme's color, like it should.
